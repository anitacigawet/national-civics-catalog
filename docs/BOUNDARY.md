# Catalog boundary

The Civic Source Catalog contains **continuing public meeting sources**.

A source belongs when the same URL helps someone find more than one meeting or public record over time. Examples include a meeting calendar, agenda or minutes index, public-notices page, video archive, RSS feed, iCalendar feed, or public meeting API.

These do not belong:

- a page for one meeting;
- one agenda, minutes file, or recording;
- meeting rows, dates, titles, transcripts, quotations, or summaries;
- parser code, selectors, request recipes, credentials, logs, or operator notes; or
- unpublished research candidates.

## Publisher and coverage

The publisher is the body that publishes the source or authorizes the service. The `covers` list names the place or jurisdiction represented by that source.

Those are not always the same thing. A county source can cover an unincorporated community. A community council can represent a place without being its government. A Tribal government can span several states. The record keeps those distinctions instead of forcing every source into a city-shaped label.

Inclusion means the URL is first-party to, or an authorized service for, the named publisher. It does not establish legal authority, government status, endorsement, completeness, or continuing availability.

## Application boundary

Applications can use the catalog in any way their license and the catalog license permit. Their parsers, databases, meeting records, generated material, and user interfaces belong in their own repositories. Z-SPAN is one such application and maintains its parsers separately.
